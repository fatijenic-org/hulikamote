This form is intended to gather the assessment of Land Transportation Office (LTO) personnel regarding the HULI-KAMOTE system, based on its simulated testing and demonstration. It is composed of two parts: Part I evaluates the system using the FURPS model (Functionality, Usability, Reliability, Performance, and Supportability), and Part II evaluates the system's perceived usability using the System Usability Scale (SUS). Kindly answer both parts as honestly and objectively as possible, as your assessment will be used to validate and further improve the system.

Part I. FURPS Evaluation

Rate each item using the scale below:
  • 4 = Highly Acceptable
  • 3 = Acceptable
  • 2 = Needs Revision
  • 1 = Not Acceptable
CriteriaRating
F – Functionality
The system accurately detects helmet non-compliance among motorcycle riders.
The system accurately detects other defined violations (e.g., missing side mirror, counterflow driving, illegal turning, passenger overloading).
The system correctly reads and records the license plate of the vehicle involved in a detected violation.
The captured evidence (frame, plate number, and violation details) would be sufficient to support a non-contact citation, similar to existing no-contact apprehension programs.
U – Usability
The administrative dashboard is easy to navigate and understand.
Violation logs and captured evidence frames are presented clearly and are easy to interpret.
The information displayed (timestamp, plate number, violation type, and evidence frame) is sufficient for documentation purposes.
R – Reliability
The simulated violations and results presented are realistic and representative of actual road and traffic conditions.
The system performs consistently across the different simulated lighting and traffic conditions shown.
The rate of false detections (false positives or false negatives) observed is within an acceptable level.
P – Performance
The system processes video footage and produces results within a reasonable amount of time.
S – Supportability
The handling of captured footage (e.g., faces, plate numbers) shows adequate consideration for data privacy.
Access to the dashboard and violation records appears properly restricted and secured.
The system's logging and recording method appears suitable for integration into the office's enforcement workflow.
Overall Acceptability
Overall, the system shows strong potential as a tool for future automated, non-contact traffic enforcement in Zamboanga City.
