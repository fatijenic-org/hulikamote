How a violation is decided

The system does not use one big model to say 'violation'. A violation is a decision built from three layers: models that find what is in the picture, tracking that follows each vehicle over time, and a small rule (a heuristic) that turns the picture and the path into a yes or no. Each of the 20 violations is its own small file in hulikamote_backend/engine/violations/ with the same shape, so any one of them can be read, tested, switched off or replaced alone.

The most important caveat of this page

No violation has been scored on real footage yet. Every rule has automated tests on scripted paths and boxes, and the benchmark tool to score them on real clips is built, but its clip list is still empty. 'Rule + tests' means the logic does what it is written to do; it does not yet mean it is accurate on Zamboanga roads. The five rules added after the panel consultation were never seen on real footage at all.

Using the dashboard

How to set up an analysis

Violations are switched on and set up in the dashboard's Forensic Analyzer wizard. The same settings are saved in camera_config.json, which the live monitor reads too.

  1. Video. Choose the clip. Turn on Keep the uploaded video if you want to harvest pictures or benchmark it later.
  2. Violations. Tick the violations to look for. Each shows what it needs: Draw a zone, Set a rider limit or Model not trained yet.
  3. Rules & zones. For each violation, draw what it asks for on the video's first frame (the hint is written in the editor). Draw one zone per place; some violations also take an arrow, a stop line, a light box, a time limit or a list of allowed vehicle types.
  4. Settings. Choose the tracker (DeepSORT, not the ByteTrack default), the plate model, plate format (ph_standard), reader, enhancement, evidence photo gap, and automatic flow detection.
  5. Run. Start the analysis. The result is a list of tickets with evidence photos for a person to review; Use for Live Monitor sends the same setup to the live view.

The camera must be right for the rules to be right
  • Fixed camera: no panning, zooming or hand-held footage (zones and the learned flow assume one view).
  • Mounted 3 to 8 m above the road, looking down 15 to 45 degrees, not straight down.
  • Vehicles at least 120 px wide for plate, helmet and seatbelt checks (the plate check ignores vehicles under 60 px).
  • At least 720p and 15 fps (25 to 30 fps is better); daylight for the first test set.
  • Each vehicle visible for 3 seconds or more (swerving needs 6).
  • No heavy rain, lens dirt or strong backlight in the main test set.

These are working values for this study and are tightened or relaxed by the benchmark results. Each card below says which view its violation needs.

Live demonstration

The same pipeline runs on a live feed. The camera input accepts a recorded file, a webcam or an RTSP address, the protocol IP cameras and CCTV recorders use. For the defense, a second laptop or a phone publishes the test footage as an RTSP stream and the system reads it exactly as it would a real camera; when a CCTV recorder exists, only the address changes. All accuracy numbers are measured on recorded footage.

The shared building blocks

Every violation uses the same few pieces. They are shown here once, with the code, so each card below can stay short.

1. The ledger: a notebook for each vehicle

For each tracked vehicle the ledger keeps its recent positions, its class, its votes and its suspicions. Every zone test uses the vehicle's ground point, the bottom-centre of its box, because that is where the tyres touch the road, and the zones are drawn on the road.

4. Votes and pending: why one wrong frame never makes a ticket

A model can be wrong on a single frame. So single-frame checks (helmet, seatbelt, phone, mirror, overloading, passengers) use a vote over the last 10 looks, and the no-plate suspicion is pending until the vehicle leaves. If the suspicion is cleared, its evidence photos are deleted.

The 12 zone rules

Judged from the vehicle's path inside zones the operator draws. They need only the vehicle detector and the tracker.

Counterflow

counterflowiZone ruleiIn the thesis list!Rule + tests; not scored on real footage

What it means

A vehicle drives against the legal direction of traffic.

Where it comes from in law

RA 4136 Section 37 (driving on the right side of the road) and the one-way rules of the LGU or MMDA.

How it is decided, step by step

  1. The detector finds the vehicle and the tracker gives it an ID; the ledger records its path (the ground point at every update).
  2. The rule computes where the vehicle is heading over the last 3 seconds or so.
  3. It compares that with the legal direction: an arrow the operator drew (zone mode), or the direction the traffic itself taught the system for that part of the picture (automatic mode).
  4. If the vehicle keeps heading against it, the vehicle is flagged and evidence frames are saved.

Models and parts involved

Model or partHow it is used here
Vehicle detectorfinds and boxes the vehicle every frame
Trackerkeeps one ID so a path can be built

How to set it up

  1. Zone mode: draw a polygon over the lane, then an arrow in the legal direction.
  2. Automatic mode (no drawing): tick Detect traffic flow automatically. The first 60 seconds of the clip are scanned to learn the flow before judging. Drawn zones, if any, still win.

Configuration: regions_of_interest.counterflow_zones = [{coordinates, legal_flow_vector{x_dir, y_dir}}] · flow_mode = zones | auto · flow_prescan_seconds (default 60)

Camera view neededAny fixed view where the direction of the road is clear.
How it is tested16 automated tests touch this file; 92.3% of its lines are run by them (tests index, 2026-10-27 13:31).
  • auto counterflow flags a car that keeps going the wrong way
  • auto counterflow needs the wrong way to last
  • auto counterflow ignores a parked car
  • auto counterflow never judges what has not been learned

The numbers (the heuristic)

What is measuredValueWhy
Position updates needed4A stable heading needs a few points.
Travel needed6 pxA parked car's box jitter is ignored.
Zone mode: against the arrowmore than 90 degrees (dot product below 0)Opposite to the arrow.
Automatic mode: against the cell's directionmore than 135 degrees, for 1.5 s in a rowStrict, so a swerve is not counterflow.

Votes per vehicle and cellat most 3One slow car cannot outvote real traffic.

Limits and honest notes

  • Two-way roads are never judged in automatic mode: both directions are normal there.
  • Needs a fixed camera; a clip whose first vehicles are the wrong-way ones learns the wrong flow.
  • Automatic learning was scored against hand-drawn zones only on the short local clips; FlowField.compare_with_zones does that check.

Illegal parking

illegal_parkingiZone ruleiIn the thesis list!Rule + tests; not scored on real footage

What it means

A vehicle stays stopped inside a no-parking zone for longer than the limit.

Where it comes from in law

RA 4136 Sections 46 and 47, and Zamboanga City Ordinance 248 (the Revised Traffic Code): no parking on crosswalks, at corners, hydrants or driveways, or where a sign says so.

How it is decided, step by step

  1. Track the vehicle and record its speed from its recent path.
  2. While its ground point is inside the zone and it is slower than 25 pixels per second, a stopwatch runs.
  3. If it moves, or leaves the zone, the stopwatch resets.
  4. When the stopwatch passes the zone's time limit the vehicle is flagged.

Models and parts involved

Model or partHow it is used here
Vehicle detectorfinds the vehicle
Trackerkeeps its ID so the stopwatch belongs to one car

How to set it up

  1. Draw a polygon over the no-parking area.
  2. Set how long a vehicle may stop there (the wizard's default is 10 s; if a zone has no value, the configuration default is 180 s).

Configuration: regions_of_interest.illegal_parking_zones = [{coordinates, time_limit}]

Camera view neededAny fixed view where the zone and the whole vehicle are visible.
How it is tested10 automated tests touch this file; 94.3% of its lines are run by them (tests index, 2026-10-27 13:31).
  • illegal parking uses the zones own limit
  • illegal parking timer restarts after the car moves
  • illegal parking flags car stopped past the limit
  • illegal parking ignores a short stop

The numbers (the heuristic)

What is measuredValueWhy
Stopped meansslower than 25 px/sA parked car's box still jitters a little; a moving car is far faster.
Position updates needed5A brand-new track is not judged on nothing.
Time limitset per zoneZone-specific.

Limits and honest notes

  • A shaking camera makes a parked car look as if it moves, hiding it.
  • A stop at a red light inside the zone also counts as stopped; draw the zone where no queue forms.

Restricted lane

restricted_laneiZone rule–Extra: not in the thesis list!Rule + tests; not scored on real footage

What it means

A vehicle type that is not allowed in a reserved lane, such as a car in a bus lane.

Where it comes from in law

Not in RA 4136. It comes from MMDA and LGU lane rules (for example bus or motorcycle lanes). Not in the thesis list; kept as an extra.

How it is decided, step by step

  1. The vehicle's class is the most common class over its whole track, so one wrong frame cannot decide.
  2. If its ground point is inside the lane and its class is not in the lane's allowed list, it is flagged.

Models and parts involved

Vehicle detectorgives the class (car, bus, motorcycle, truck)
Trackerkeeps the ID so the class is voted over the track

How to set it up

  1. Draw a polygon over the reserved lane.
  2. Tick which vehicle types are allowed (default: bus). Leaving the list empty closes the lane to everyone.

Configuration: regions_of_interest.restricted_lane_zones = [{coordinates, allowed_classes}]

    Illegal turn

    illegal_turniZone ruleiIn the thesis list!Rule + tests; not scored on real footage

    What it means

    A vehicle turns in a direction that is not allowed at a junction.

    Where it comes from in law

    RA 4136 Section 45 (turning at intersections) and 'No turn' signs.

    How it is decided, step by step

    1. Track the vehicle's heading as it enters the zone, then its heading now.
    2. A turn is a swing of at least 45 degrees.
    3. If it now points along the forbidden arrow (within 45 degrees) and did not point that way when it entered, it is flagged. Driving straight along the arrow is not a turn.

    Illegal U-turn

    illegal_u_turniZone ruleiAdded after the consultation!New; tested on scripted paths only

    What it means

    A vehicle turns right round where a U-turn is prohibited.

    Where it comes from in law

    To confirm. There is no single U-turn offence: it is charged under the sign or marking that prohibits it (disregarding traffic signs or illegal turn), or as reckless driving (Section 48) or obstruction (Section 54).

    How it is decided, step by step

    1. Learn the heading after entering the zone and the heading now.
    2. A U-turn is a swing of 150 degrees or more and a sideways shift of at least 0.75 box widths from the entry line.
    3. A vehicle that only reverses in a straight line has no sideways shift, so it is not flagged.

    Swerving

    swervingiZone ruleiIn the thesis list!Rule + tests; not scored on real footage

    What it means

    A vehicle weaves from side to side.

    Where it comes from in law

    RA 4136 Section 48 (reckless driving). Swerving is not named in the law: it is our measurable proxy, and we say so.

    How it is decided, step by step

    1. Take the vehicle's path over the last 6 seconds and smooth it.
    2. Measure each point's sideways offset from the straight line between the first and last point.
    3. Count back-and-forth swings that are at least half a box width. Two or more reversals (left-right-left) flag the vehicle.

    Crossing a solid line

    crossing_solid_lineiZone ruleiAdded after the consultation!New; tested on scripted paths only

    What it means

    A vehicle crosses a solid line that must not be crossed (for example a double yellow line).

    Where it comes from in law

    To confirm. RA 4136 Section 41 (restrictions on overtaking and passing). The double-solid-line rule is usually quoted from road-marking guides, not from the statute text.

    How it is decided, step by step

    1. The first time the vehicle is clearly on one side of the line, that side is remembered.
    2. When its ground point ends up clearly on the other side, the vehicle is flagged once.
    3. A car wobbling on its own side is not a crossing: it has to end up past the line by 30% of its width (at least 10 px).

      What it means

      Where it comes from in law

      Terms used on this page

      On screen, hover or tab to any dotted word for its meaning. This list is the same meanings, for the printed copy.

      CCTV
      Closed-circuit television: fixed security or traffic cameras.
      dot product
      A number that tells whether two arrows point the same way (positive), at right angles (zero) or opposite ways (negative).
      grid cell
      One small square of the picture when it is cut into a regular grid.
      ground point
      The bottom-centre of a vehicle's box, where its tyres touch the road. All zone tests use this point.
      heuristic
      A rule of thumb with numbers somebody chose and measured. It gives a good-enough answer without a learned model.
      Hz
      Times per second. A light that blinks at 1 Hz switches on and off once every second.
      JAO 2014-01
      Joint Administrative Order 2014-01: the uniform list of traffic violation names and fines used on tickets.
      ledger
      Our notebook for one vehicle: its positions over time, its votes, its class and what it is suspected of.
      LGU
      Local Government Unit: a city or municipality, such as Zamboanga City.
      MMDA
      Metropolitan Manila Development Authority. Its traffic rules apply in Metro Manila.
      pending
      A suspicion that is not final yet. It becomes a ticket only if it still holds when the vehicle leaves the picture.
      RA 4136
      Republic Act 4136, the Land Transportation and Traffic Code of 1964. The base law for most of our violations.
      RTSP
      Real Time Streaming Protocol: the way IP cameras and CCTV recorders send live video over a network.
      vote
      Deciding from many looks instead of one: for example 'bad in at least 60% of the last 10 looks'.

      The code editor many developers use. The 'Open in VS Code' links jump straight to the line.
