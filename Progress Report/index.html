1 · Goal and scope

A prototype that shows contactless detection is technically feasible with local tools

What the thesis commits to

  • Train and validate detection models; software around them (backend, dashboard, visualizer) is required but secondary.
  • Three-stage pipeline: YOLO detection, DeepSORT tracking, EasyOCR plate reading with preprocessing.
  • All testing on pre-recorded footage. Live cameras are supported by the architecture but not part of the study.
  • Human operators review every flag. No autonomous legal decisions.

How success is measured

  • mAP@50: detection accuracy at 50% box overlap.
  • Logging and organization accuracy rate: is the violation type, plate, vehicle identity and evidence frame recorded correctly for a ground-truth event?
  • False positive and false negative rates, tracked separately.

Motorcycle (8)

Counterflow, illegal parking, illegal turn, disregarding signs, no plate, no helmet, missing side mirror, overloading

Car (5)

Counterflow, illegal parking, illegal turn, disregarding signs, no plate

Extended, stretch goals (5)

Signal light, red light running, swerving, no seatbelt, pedestrian lane obstruction

Unique violation types across the three lists: 13. The system also implements restricted lane (bus lane), which is not in the thesis list.

2 · Legal sources · context for the whole thesis

Non-contact apprehension in the courts

AUG 2022

Supreme Court TRO

The Court restrained the No Contact Apprehension Policy of the MMDA and several Metro Manila cities.

MAY 2025

Partly lifted for the MMDA

The TRO was lifted for MMDA enforcement on major roads such as EDSA and C5. Enforcement resumed on May 26, 2025. City ordinances were not covered.

MID 2026

Petitions dismissed as moot

The Court dismissed the consolidated petitions: a unified Metro Manila traffic code changed the situation, and the petitioners had procedural problems. It did not rule that NCAP is valid. Reports give different dates (June 3 or July 9).

Issues the petitioners raised

  • Privacy: anyone could look up violations with a plate number.
  • Conditions such as blocking registration renewal until fines are paid.
  • Due process for owners who were not the driver.

What it means for this thesis

  • We study the detection technology, not the legal power to fine.
  • A human reviews every flag; owners are not notified automatically.
  • Plate corrections and masked personal data answer the privacy concern in part.

From news reports (GMA News, Philstar, Tribune, Top Gear). Confirm the G.R. numbers and the decision date on the Supreme Court website before quoting.

SAY The panel may ask if this is even allowed. Our answer: it is a research prototype on recorded footage, with a person reviewing every flag. The courts have not settled the policy question; the last ruling did not decide validity. We name that risk and stay inside our scope.

2 · What changed since Aug 25

How a ticket is decided

1 · Never trust one frame

Model-based checks vote over the last 10 looks. A single wrong guess cannot create a ticket.

2 · Judge fairly

A plate look only counts if the vehicle is big enough, its class has settled and, for motorcycles, the rear faces the camera.

3 · Decide at the end

No plate is a suspicion while the car is on screen and becomes a ticket only if no plate was found by the time it leaves. Photos of cleared suspicions are deleted.

4 · Show why

Evidence photos carry the zone, the vehicle box and the part that caused the flag: the bare head, the phone, the plate area searched.

5 · Human decides

The reviewer confirms, rejects or corrects the plate. Merge and "different car" decisions become ReID training data.

6 · Learn from it

Rejected tickets and corrections feed the harvest inbox, so the next training round targets the model's real mistakes.

SAY This is the answer to "how do you control false positives". Voting, fair-look rules, end-of-track decisions and human review are four independent layers.
2 · What changed since Aug 25

Problems we found in our own system

Tracker box format

DeepSORT expects centre boxes; we sent top-left boxes. One car got several IDs: a test run made 29 tickets with track 19 twice. Fixed, plus a stitcher that rejoins broken tracks.

Same photo in train and test

Two public sets (cabin, vehicles) had pixel-identical photos on both sides. About 1,600 copies were moved into training so test scores are not inflated.

A dataset we rejected

A 6,608-image helmet set had only 21 "no helmet" labels in training and none in validation or test, mostly product photos. Not used.

No-plate was too eager

It fired after two misses and never cleared. It now needs 6 fair looks, clears when a plate appears, and is decided when the car leaves.

Speed

1080p stutter fixed with a 720p working size and round-robin specialist scheduling (about 3x FPS). ONNX Runtime pinned to the discrete GPU.

Model input mismatch

The app feeds models differently from how they were trained. Measured on the next slide.

SAY This is the slide that earns trust. Three findings came from measuring, not guessing. Be clear that the input-mismatch fix is not in the backend yet and that the 85% is on the plate set's own domain, not on Zamboanga footage.

What each term means

  • Files: image files in that split, including copies made by augmentation (flips, blur, brightness).
  • Unique photos: real original pictures, after grouping the copies. This is the honest size of the data.
  • Copies per photo: files divided by unique photos. About 3 means each photo was used 3 times.
  • Objects: labelled boxes. One picture can hold many (a street with 5 cars has 5 objects).
  • Train / validation / test: learn from it, check during training, final exam.

What to notice

  • Validation and test together are only 6% to 13% of each set, so their scores carry more uncertainty.
  • Zero background-only pictures in every set: the models almost never see "nothing here", which invites false alarms. The harvest tool adds these (key N).
  • Vehicles are lopsided: 27,168 people, 4,721 tricycles, 4,945 motorcycles.
  • Helmet is balanced (2,927 with, 3,177 without). Cabin has no tinted window and no side mirror examples.

How it learns

  • Transfer learning: start from weights that already know 80 everyday object types (COCO), replace the last layer with our classes, then train on our photos.
  • Each epoch: predict, measure the three losses against the labels, adjust the weights a little (backpropagation).
  • Validation photos are only measured, never learned from.

Why YOLO and why nano

  • One pass over the picture gives every box: fast enough for video.
  • Nano: 3.01 million parameters and 8.2 GFLOPs at 640 px. At 320 px a specialist costs about a quarter of that.
  • YOLO11 and YOLO26 follow the same idea; YOLO26 is built to skip the NMS step.

4 · Models and results · architecture

Tracking and plate reading

DeepSORT: one ID per vehicle

  1. Predict: a Kalman filter guesses where each known vehicle should be in this frame.
  2. Match: new boxes are matched to tracks using position and appearance. Appearance is a 512-number fingerprint made from the box crop (64×128 px); similar fingerprints mean the same car.
  3. Update: matched tracks move; unmatched boxes start new tracks.
  4. Confirm and expire: a track needs 3 confirmed frames to get an ID, and survives up to 90 frames without a match.

Our settings: appearance distance 0.4, overlap 0.7, minimum confidence 0.1, memory of 100 fingerprints per track. Our stitcher also rejoins a broken track by colour signature and timing.

Plate: from crop to text

  1. Find: the plate specialist boxes the plate inside the vehicle crop.
  2. Crop: cut the plate with 10% padding. Keep up to 40 crops per vehicle, each with its time.
  3. Sharpen: Real-ESRGAN enlarges the crop and rebuilds detail.
  4. Read: EasyOCR finds the text and recognises letters; only A-Z and 0-9 are allowed.
  5. Fix by position: in the PH format the first three characters are letters and the last four are digits, so a 0 in a letter slot becomes O.
  6. Vote: across all crops, the text with the most reads wins, ties broken by confidence, with a bonus for the right length. No read means "unidentified", never a blank.
Why vote: one blurry frame misreads a plate (O for 0, B for 8). Many frames voted together fix most single-frame mistakes.
SAY Two small stories. The tracker answers "is this the same car as a second ago", using where it should be and what it looks like. The plate chain answers "what does the plate say", and it votes across many frames, because any single frame can be wrong. These two are why one car becomes one ticket with one plate.
4 · Models and results

How the models were trained

Setup

  • Method: transfer learning from COCO-pretrained YOLOv8n weights (nano size, built for a 6 GB GPU).
  • Hardware: NVIDIA RTX 3050 Laptop GPU, 6 GB, Windows 11.
  • Software: Ultralytics 8.4, PyTorch 2.5.1 (CUDA 12.1), ONNX Runtime GPU, Python 3.12.
  • Schedule: up to 100 epochs (vehicles: 30), batch 16, early stop after 30 epochs without improvement.
  • Augmentation: mosaic, HSV shifts, flips, small rotation and scale, on top of the augmented copies already in the public sets.
  • Output: best-epoch weights exported to ONNX and registered in the model catalog.

Why this design

  • Specialists on crops instead of one many-class detector: smaller models, faster, retrainable one at a time, and each can be compared on its own.
  • Nano model: real-time on laptop hardware. Larger models are an option if scores fall short.
  • Reproducible runs: every run folder keeps its settings (args.yaml) and per-epoch scores (results.csv); data sources are listed in a manifest.
  • Pause and resume from the dashboard or the terminal Training Lab, because the GPU is shared with other work.
Note for the record: helmet, cabin and plate v1 were trained at 640 px, but the pipeline feeds specialists 320 px crops and exports them at 320 px. Scores must be re-checked at 320 before they are quoted as field numbers.

7 · Questions to expect · method

Method

6 · Why a cascade of specialists instead of one detector with every class?ready

Smaller models on small crops are fast on a laptop GPU, can be retrained and compared one at a time, and avoid class imbalance across very different objects (plates versus helmets).

Trade-off: errors compound (a missed vehicle means no helmet check). We will test it with an ablation against a single multi-class model if time allows.

7 · Why YOLOv8n, and why compare YOLO11 and YOLO26?ready

The nano size runs in real time on a 6 GB GPU. The three generations are all supported in the pipeline, so the comparison changes only the detector, with identical clips and rules.

8 · Most violations are geometry rules. Where is the AI?ready

Detection, tracking appearance, plate reading, helmet and cabin checks are learned. The violations themselves are traffic definitions: "counterflow" is, by definition, movement against the legal direction, so a deterministic rule on a learned track is the correct tool. Thresholds are tuned on clips and the rules are tested with scripted tracks.

9 · How do you deal with the same car getting several IDs?ready

We found a real bug (box format sent to DeepSORT) that produced duplicate tickets, fixed it, and added a stitcher that rejoins broken tracks. Reviewers can merge or separate cars, and those decisions become ReID data. ID switches per clip will be reported in the benchmark.

10 · How do you control false positives?ready

Four layers: votes over several looks, fair-look rules (size, class, direction), end-of-track decisions, and human review. Rule tests include false-positive traps such as a car waiting on a stop line.

SAY The strongest answer here is 8. Do not apologise for rules: say that a definition is the right place for a rule and that the learning sits where perception is hard.

Appendix D

Glossary in plain words

mAP@50

How well boxes match real objects, counting a box correct if it overlaps the true box by at least half. 1.00 is perfect.

mAP@50-95

The same, averaged over stricter overlaps up to 95%. Low values mean loose boxes.

Precision ("trust")

Of the things the model flagged, how many were real.

Recall ("coverage")

Of the real things, how many the model found.

Validation versus test

Validation photos are checked during training and pick the best epoch. Test photos are kept for one final exam.

Letterbox

Resizing a picture without stretching it, padding the empty sides, which is how the detectors were trained.

ReID

A small model that tells whether two photos show the same vehicle, used to keep one ID per car.

Transfer learning

Starting from a model that already learned everyday objects, then teaching it our classes.

SAY For panel members outside computer vision.
